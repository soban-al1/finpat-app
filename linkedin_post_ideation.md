# LinkedIn Post — Ideation with AI

---

One of the things I've always believed is that domain knowledge compounds.

Every team I've worked with, every product decision I've sat in on, every user problem I've had to untangle — it all stacks up. And for the first time, I feel like I've found a way to actually deploy that compound in a structured, accelerated way. That's AI as a PM tool.

When I started building FinPat, I didn't open a design tool. I didn't write a user story. I sat down and wrote a product brief, word by word.

And I mean that literally. Every sentence had to earn its place. What problem are we solving? For whom, exactly? What does "solved" look like? What does the user feel when they use this? I kept pushing until the ambiguity was gone — not reduced, gone. AI became my thinking partner through that process. It pushed back, asked questions, exposed the gaps I was skating over. The kind of friction that usually takes a great team weeks to produce, I was getting in sessions.

What surprised me most was what happened during that clarity work: I started pivoting.

Not because the original idea was bad — but because the clearer I got on the problem, the more I could see where my initial instincts were slightly off. The brief wasn't just documentation. It was ideation. It forced me to be honest about what I was actually building versus what I thought I was building. By the time I had a brief I was truly happy with, the product had already evolved.

From there, breaking it into incremental value became almost natural. When you know the destination clearly, the waypoints reveal themselves. What's the smallest version of this that's meaningful? What comes first because it unlocks everything else? That conversation with AI — grounded in years of PM judgment — moved fast.

Then came the product spec. And something shifted. The work started to feel structured, tangible, close. The spec isn't just a plan — it's the moment things stop being ideas and start being real. By the end of it, I could see the design phase clearly. I knew what we were building and why. The designer wasn't going to need to guess at intent.

If you're a PM and you haven't tried using AI as a rigorous thinking partner — not just a writing assistant, not a shortcut — I'd encourage you to try it from the very first sentence of your brief. Bring your experience into the conversation. The compounding is the point.
